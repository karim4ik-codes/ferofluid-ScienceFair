What is inside?

Ferrofluid is a mix of three things. Typical amounts by volume:

≈5%

Magnetic nanoparticles

Magnetite (Fe₃O₄), about 10 nm wide. 10,000 of them in a row would be about as wide as a hair.

≈10%

Surfactant coat

A soapy layer around each particle so they don't clump together into big lumps.

≈85%

Carrier liquid

Usually an oil. The particles float in it thanks to Brownian motion and never sink.

Why do spikes appear?

The particles line up along the magnetic field lines and pull the liquid upward. Other forces pull it back. The spikes are the shape where these forces balance. Scientists call it the Rosensweig instability.

Magnetic force

↓ pulls down

Gravity and surface tension

They try to keep the liquid flat and smooth.

Take the magnet away and the liquid becomes flat again at once. It is superparamagnetic: magnetic only while a magnet is near.

From rockets to speakers

1963Steve Papell, an engineer at NASA, invents ferrofluid to move rocket fuel in zero gravity.
1960s–70sRonald Rosensweig studies it and explains the spike patterns.
TodayIt works inside loudspeakers, machines and computer parts, and doctors study magnetic nanoparticles for medicine.

Where is it used?

Loudspeakers

Cools the coil and makes the sound cleaner.

Machines

Forms liquid seals that keep dust out.

Medicine

Research on carrying medicine to one exact spot.

Art

Moving sculptures controlled by magnets.

Fun facts

Space origin. It was invented for rockets, not for science fairs.

Too hot to be magnetic. Heat it enough and it loses its magnetism. Try the temperature slider above.

It can climb. Put a magnet under a metal bolt and the liquid crawls up it like a spiral.

Very messy. It stains almost everything, so scientists keep it in sealed glass.
